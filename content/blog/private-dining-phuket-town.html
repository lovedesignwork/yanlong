For private dining in Phuket Town, start with the people and the purpose of the meal. Yan Long has private rooms for small gatherings and larger celebrations, with Chinese dishes designed to share. The useful first decision is not which room looks most impressive: it is how many guests need to sit comfortably, what kind of conversation you want, and how the meal should unfold.

A clear enquiry helps the restaurant suggest the right arrangement. It also keeps the host from discovering important details only after invitations have gone out.

Match the room to the real headcount

Yan Long's published information describes three Boutique Rooms accommodating up to ten guests each and a Grand Room accommodating up to fifty. Treat those figures as a starting point for discussion. Your preferred seating, the number of children and any space needed for movement may affect the practical arrangement.

Give the team both your expected number and the largest number you might reasonably reach. A gathering expected to have eight guests but possibly twelve needs a different conversation from a confirmed party of eight. Do not assume that rooms can be combined or extra chairs added without checking.

View the private dining information, then ask which room is available for your occasion. A photograph shows a setting; the restaurant's confirmation establishes the room and arrangement you will actually receive.

Explain what privacy means for this meal

A family reunion may need room for several conversations at once. A business dinner may need a calm setting where everybody can follow one discussion. A birthday meal may require a little coordination around a surprise. These are different needs even when the party size is identical.

  • For a business gathering, explain whether you need a meal with conversation or a formal presentation.
  • For a family occasion, mention children and guests who may need easier access.
  • For a celebration, describe any planned moment that affects service.
  • For any event, confirm room-use time and the seating arrangement before inviting guests.

Ask about equipment, sound and privacy if they matter to your plans. Do not infer audio-visual facilities, soundproofing or exclusive use of adjacent areas from the words “private dining.” A room can be ideal for a meal without serving as a meeting venue.

Understand the full booking arrangement

Private-room planning is easier when the financial details are explicit. Ask for the current minimum spend, what counts toward it, whether any room charge applies, and how taxes or service charges are handled. Published figures can help you prepare, but a written quotation should guide the actual booking.

Also confirm any deposit, payment timing, cancellation terms and the deadline for a final headcount. These are questions to ask, not policies assumed by this guide. Keep the restaurant's response with the booking details so the person handling payment has the same information as the host.

Build a shared menu with enough variety

Choose the main direction of the meal before adding individual favourites. Will Peking duck be the centerpiece? Does your group particularly enjoy seafood? Are you planning a lighter business lunch or a generous family dinner? One clear direction makes the rest of the menu easier to balance.

Combine richer dishes with vegetables and simpler accompaniments. Vary the cooking methods as well as the ingredients: a table full of crisp roast dishes can feel repetitive even when each one is excellent. Ask the kitchen to advise on quantities for the confirmed party size.

For a menu starting point, use our guide to Yan Long's signature dishes. Whole roast specialties may require advance arrangements. Check the actual lead time for the dish you want rather than assuming a table reservation also reserves the food.

Collect individual needs before the final order

Send guests one short message asking about allergies, ingredients they avoid and any access needs. This is more reliable than making assumptions based on age, nationality or a previous meal together. Share the relevant information with the restaurant before settling the menu.

For a shared meal, ask how separate dishes will be identified and served. Some guests may be comfortable with a separate plate; others need a more detailed conversation about ingredients and preparation. The restaurant should confirm what it can accommodate.

Choose one contact person for changes. When several guests independently call about the same booking, it becomes harder to know which headcount or menu is current. A single organiser can keep everyone aligned without making the meal feel formal.

Send an enquiry the team can act on

A useful message might read: “We are planning a private family dinner for eight adults and two children. We would like a shared Chinese menu with Peking duck, and one guest avoids shellfish. Please advise on a suitable room, current minimum spend, menu options and what you need from us to confirm.”

Add your preferred day, arrival time and contact details. If the occasion has a firm finish time, mention it too. Keep optional extras separate from essential requirements so the team can propose a workable plan.

Private dining questions

Does a room request confirm the booking?

No. Wait for the restaurant to confirm availability and the agreed arrangements. The enquiry begins the conversation; it does not replace a confirmed room allocation.

Can I organise a birthday or business meal?

Both are among the occasions Yan Long describes for its private rooms. Our birthday planning guide and business lunch guide cover the different decisions involved. When you are ready, request your table or room with a concise outline of the gathering.
